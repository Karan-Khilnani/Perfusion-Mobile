---
name: Clinical Advisory history
description: Product terminology and integrity rules for signed Clinical Advisories and follow-up reviews.
---

Use “Clinical Advisory” or “Advisory” everywhere users or the public can see the platform’s specialist opinion. Preserve existing prescription-named routes, database fields, and other internal identifiers for backward compatibility. Keep the initial signed Clinical Advisory and every follow-up review as separate immutable records. Seeker-facing views must present them as one chronological trail, and the cumulative PDF must include every record through the latest review. In Case File Chat, keep advisory IDs available internally but render only the human-readable narrative from structured advisory references. Legacy drafts must never be promoted into Advisory history: migrate only finalized records with confirmed Provider provenance.

**Why:** Perfusion provides clinical opinions to the treating team rather than acting as prescriber of record. Internal renaming would risk breaking historical links and integrations. Overwriting records would hide follow-up care and break the permanent history. Treating a draft or seeker-attributed fallback as signed clinical advice would corrupt the medico-legal record.

**How to apply:** Avoid user-facing “Prescription,” “Rx,” or prescribing language except when explicitly saying an advisory is not a prescription. Keep draft → sign/lock, timestamps, verification, individual PDFs, and the complete ordered trail intact. Normalize structured advisory references at the rendering boundary without mutating stored/API metadata; ordinary chat text and attachment renderers remain on their existing paths. Backfills and compatibility migrations must require finalization and a resolvable Provider author; skip ambiguous records rather than guessing.
